Research Scope Approved

Section feedback

FormForge is treated as a fresh-start AI-assisted form builder prototype. The initial product loop is:

  1. A creator describes the form they need.
  2. AI drafts a usable form structure.
  3. The creator reviews and edits fields visually.
  4. The creator publishes a public link.
  5. Respondents submit the form.
  6. The creator reviews or exports responses.

Non-goals: do not rebuild the old app feature-for-feature; do not make billing, teams, API access, webhooks, durable file storage, provider integrations, or full i18n part of the first validated loop; do not select implementation architecture, schema, or UI spec; do not write canonical research/icp.md until Stage 3 approval.

Business Model And Side Coverage

Section feedback

FormForge is not a marketplace. The likely model is B2B SaaS with a product-led/self-serve motion and a prosumer edge.

Buyer / Creator

The person or small team that creates and reviews forms.

Operator / User

Often the same person in solo businesses; sometimes an assistant, coordinator, or operations teammate.

Respondent

Clients, leads, attendees, volunteers, customers, applicants, or community members who fill the form. Respondents influence value through completion quality but are not the paying ICP.

Excluded Side

Respondents are not ranked as ICP candidates because they do not buy FormForge. Their needs should be captured inside buyer value drivers, especially mobile completion, trust, clarity, and error handling.

Executive Findings

Section feedback

1. Primary ICP Candidate

Claim: Client-service solo operators and small service teams with repeated intake/onboarding needs have the best current combination of urgency, accessibility, repeated form variation, and willingness to pay.

Evidence: HoneyBook and Dubsado organize value around client relationships, lead capture, questionnaires, proposals, contracts, invoices, onboarding, and automation. The FormForge loop can produce value before full CRM replacement when scoped to intake/questionnaire creation plus response review/export.

Inference: This segment repeatedly needs forms that differ by service, offer, cohort, project type, or client context. The buyer is close to the work and submitted data affects paid delivery quality.

Confidence: Medium-high.

2. AI Alone Is Weak

Claim: FormForge should not position only around natural-language form generation.

Evidence: Typeform includes AI-powered form creation; Jotform advertises AI, templates, integrations, payments, and workflows; Tally and Fillout compete on free/unlimited usage and advanced form functionality.

Inference: AI-assisted form creation is increasingly table stakes. FormForge needs a narrower job-specific promise.

Confidence: High.

3. Lead/Quote Is Secondary

Claim: Lead/quote qualification is credible but risks turning FormForge into CRM or quote-estimation software too early.

Evidence: Client-service suites connect lead capture with proposals, contracts, payments, workflows, and follow-up; FormForge defers CRM, billing, webhooks, and integrations.

Inference: Revenue urgency is strong, but value may be blocked by follow-up automation, status, quote logic, scheduling, or CRM handoff.

Confidence: Medium.

4. Events And Nonprofits

Claim: Event/community registration and nonprofit/volunteer coordination have real pain but weaker early-fit economics.

Evidence: Event tools emphasize registration, ticketing, attendee lists, check-in, reminders, event pages, and analytics. Jotform and Fillout offer nonprofit/education discounts, and Tally's free unlimited model creates price pressure.

Confidence: Medium.

5. HR/Ops Lower Fit

Claim: Internal HR/ops is less aligned with a lightweight public-form first loop.

Evidence: Larger form tools sell HR, enterprise, security, and workflow use cases; FormForge defers permissions, compliance, routing, and integrations.

Confidence: Medium-low.

Primary Candidate: Client-Service Intake Operators

Section feedback

Who They Are

Independent consultants, coaches, fractional operators, boutique agencies, creative studios, professional service providers, personal-service providers, and small client-service teams that need structured information before a paid engagement, cohort, project, consultation, or recurring service.

Jobs To Be Done

  • Create a professional intake form quickly when a new lead or client needs to be qualified or onboarded.
  • Update intake questions when a service offer changes.
  • Review/export responses and start delivery with fewer missing details.
  • Make the first client interaction credible and easy.

Trigger Events

  • Launching a new service, cohort, workshop, package, or consulting offer.
  • Signing a new client or preparing kickoff.
  • Repeatedly receiving incomplete lead/client information by email or calls.
  • Switching away from Google Forms, Typeform, or a heavy CRM.
  • Standardizing messy manual onboarding after enough client volume creates rework.

Current Alternatives

  • Google Forms or Microsoft Forms.
  • Typeform, Jotform, Tally, Fillout, and Paperform.
  • HoneyBook, Dubsado, and similar client-flow suites.
  • Notion, Airtable, Sheets, and manual email follow-up.
  • Custom website forms or embedded contact forms.

Desired Outcomes

  • Reduce setup time.
  • Improve completeness and quality of submitted client information.
  • Make the form feel professional enough for external clients.
  • Avoid migrating into a full business-management suite too early.
  • Keep response review/export simple enough for solo operators and assistants.

Value Drivers And Risks

Value drivers are faster variable intake setup, better first impression, less manual follow-up, easier reuse/editing, and lower cognitive load than full CRM suites.

Risks are weak branding, thin response handling, AI output cleanup burden, and missing integrations for users who cannot accept export-only workflows.

Secondary Candidates

Section feedback

Lead / Quote Qualification

Strong revenue urgency; revisit when FormForge can test verticalized lead intake. Risk: high-value lead forms often require quote estimation, scheduling, CRM status, reminders, or fast follow-up automation.

Event / Community Registration

Clear form needs but lower fit with a generic first loop. Event users often need capacity controls, waitlists, rosters, reminders, ticketing/payments, attendee edits, and check-in.

Nonprofit / Volunteer Coordination

Operational pain but weaker WTP. Free/discounted tools are common, and workflows can need scheduling, donor/volunteer CRM, waivers, accessibility, and committee approval.

HR / Internal Ops

Likely too compliance- and integration-sensitive for the first lightweight public-form loop.

Alternatives And Lower-Confidence Findings

Section feedback
  • Lead/quote forms as primary: rejected for now because the value is attractive but the first product likely needs quote logic and follow-up workflow sooner than the approved loop includes.
  • Event/community registration as primary: rejected for now because event management-specific requirements can overtake form creation.
  • Nonprofits/volunteer coordinators: rejected for now because WTP and budget friction look weaker.
  • Generic individuals and small teams: rejected because it is too broad to guide discovery, messaging, or first product constraints.
  • Lower-confidence finding: client-service users may accept export-only response handling before integrations. This requires interviews.
