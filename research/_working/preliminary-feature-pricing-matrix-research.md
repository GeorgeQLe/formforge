## Methodology

This packet compares buyer-visible offer structure rather than attempting a complete feature audit. It separates observed facts from inferred gaps:

- Public prices are recorded only when visible on current public pages.
- `Not public`, `included in broader suite`, or `free/workspace bundled` is used when a direct standalone price is not visible.
- Features are weighted for the approved ICP: fast first draft, polished client-facing intake, file/logic/completion controls, downstream handoff, integrations, workflow depth, and whether the buyer must adopt a broader client-management suite.
- This is not GTM, positioning, or product planning. Implications are limited to inputs the parent synthesis should preserve or test.

## Market Boundary

FormForge is evaluated as an AI-assisted client-intake form builder for small client-service businesses. The comparison includes:

- General form builders: Typeform, Jotform, Tally, Fillout, Paperform, forms.app.
- Free/manual substitute: Google Forms inside Google Workspace.
- Client-service suites: HoneyBook, Dubsado.
- Vertical intake/practice-management proof point: IntakeQ.

## Gaps And Patterns

| Pattern | Evidence | Opportunity/Risk | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free and low-cost plans are extremely strong for generic forms. | Tally offers unlimited forms/submissions free within fair use; Fillout offers unlimited forms/seats and 1,000 responses/mo free; forms.app offers unlimited responses and AI-powered features on free; Google Forms covers basic creation, logic, charts, and Sheets export. | Risk: generic AI form creation has a low price ceiling unless FormForge proves client-intake-specific value. | High |
| AI form creation is no longer a premium differentiator by itself. | Typeform includes AI-powered form creation in Core; Paperform includes AI form creation; forms.app lists AI-powered features; HoneyBook includes HoneyBook AI. | Risk: FormForge's AI loop is useful but must be tied to better intake setup, completeness, or handoff. | High |
| Client-service suites bundle intake with the surrounding paid-client workflow. | HoneyBook includes proposals/contracts, invoices/payments, calendar, client portal, lead forms, automations, reports, and AI. Dubsado includes invoices, contracts/proposals, lead forms, client portals, scheduling, workflows, and Zapier on Premier. | Opportunity and risk: an anti-suite wedge is plausible for buyers avoiding migration, but suite buyers may prefer bundled client operations. | Medium-high |
| Specialized intake commands materially higher prices when tied to secure workflow. | IntakeQ's Forms Only plan is $54.90/mo and includes attachments, e-signatures, reminders, secure document sharing, exports, and HIPAA BAA; low-volume Forms Only is still $29.90/mo for 10 submissions/month. | Opportunity: willingness to pay rises when intake includes reminders, secure sharing, signatures, and operational handoff. | Medium |

## Preliminary Synthesis Inputs

These are inputs for the parent competitive-analysis synthesis, not final recommendations:

1. The strongest pricing pressure comes from Tally, Fillout, forms.app, and Google Forms, not Typeform alone.
2. The strongest packaging pressure comes from HoneyBook and Dubsado because they make intake part of a broader paid-client operating system.
3. The strongest WTP proof comes from specialized or workflow-rich intake, especially IntakeQ's combination of forms, attachments, signatures, reminders, secure sharing, and exports.
4. The most visible current FormForge gap is post-submission handoff/integrations, because several competitors expose Sheets, Notion, Airtable, Zapier, Make, webhooks, CRM, storage, or suite-native workflow routing.
5. AI generation should be preserved as current product capability, but the synthesis should avoid treating it as a standalone differentiator.
